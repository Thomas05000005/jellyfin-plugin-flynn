# Flynn v0.4.0

Adds **Capacity**: when your disks run out of room.

## It will say nothing for two weeks

That is the feature, not a limitation. A date drawn from three nightly readings is a guess wearing
a number, and the number is what someone deletes a library on. Fourteen readings covers two full
weekly cycles, so weekend imports and Sunday backups are inside the sample rather than defining it.

Until then the card says how many readings it has.

## What it does differently from the usual

**A median, not a line fit.** Least squares has no resistance to a single outlier, and one
overnight import of half a terabyte is exactly that outlier -- it would drag the projection
wherever it liked. The daily changes are reduced with a median instead, which one unusual night in
a month does not move.

**Four answers, not one number.** Filling gives a date. Flat or emptying says there is nothing to
predict. A trend smaller than the day-to-day scatter is reported as inconclusive rather than
dressed up as precision. Not enough history says so.

**On ZFS it aims at the reserve, not at zero.** A pool holds back space that ordinary writes
cannot touch; once free space falls into it, writes fail. Extrapolating to zero would promise time
that does not exist.

## Alerts need two things to be true

An entry appears in the inbox at ninety, thirty and seven days -- but only for a disk that is
already at least seventy percent full.

A disk at a fifth of its capacity filling quickly produces a perfectly real "full in sixty days"
that nobody needs to act on today. An alert that fires on that is the alert people learn to
ignore, and then the real one arrives and gets ignored with it.

Issues close themselves when a pool stops filling. Nothing has to remember they were raised.

## Requires

Jellyfin **12.0 or later**.
