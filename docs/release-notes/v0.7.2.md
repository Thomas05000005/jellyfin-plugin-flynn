# Flynn v0.7.2

Found by reading two weeks of real readings off a real server, not by testing.

## The forecast counted a three-hour gap as a day

The nightly task can also be run by hand, and Thomas had done so several times. Deltas are
normalised by the time actually elapsed, which is correct — a missed night must not read as a
doubling — but it cuts the other way too: a small change over a short gap becomes an enormous
daily rate.

His worst case: **1.4 GB moved over 3.3 hours, normalised to −10,412 MB/day.**

The median absorbed it, as it was built to. It still carried it:

```
median of all 14 intervals        971 MB/day     <- what the card said
median of the 11 gaps over 6 h    765 MB/day
```

27% apart. On his disk that is *full in 123 days* against *156*.

## What changed

Gaps shorter than six hours are dropped — but only when at least five longer ones remain. A server
whose task only ever runs manually still gets an answer, because a noisy forecast beats no forecast.

## The test is his data

The new test carries his fourteen real intervals, hours and rates as measured, three of them short.
It asserts the answer is 765 rather than 971.

Verified by removing the filter: `Expected: 765 / Actual: 971` — the test reproduces the exact
number that was on his screen.

## Also looked at and left alone

The card names a device node — `/dev/nvme0n1p2` — where a mount point might read better. That is
deliberate: his NVMe carries both `/config` and `/cache`, and naming one of them would suggest a
single folder was filling when the whole device is. Less pretty, more honest.
